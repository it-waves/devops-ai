Lesson 30 of the DevOps Engineering Bootcamp, the fifth lesson of the Linux administration masterclass. A process is a running program, and on a server you must find, read and stop them. You list processes with ps and choose your own columns (PID, user, CPU, memory), start a job in the background, send the TERM signal with kill, find a runaway process by sorting on CPU, and learn why kill -9 comes last. You read uptime, nproc and free -h to judge how busy the machine is (load against CPU cores, and the available memory column), and you check whether a program is running with pgrep and its exit status. You finish with common myths, a six-point pass or fail rubric and six tasks to do in your own virtual machine.

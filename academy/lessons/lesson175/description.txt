Lesson 175 of the DevOps Engineering Bootcamp. Your repository works, but can you trust it? In this lesson we make Nexus Repository trustworthy, on a real server, with a real browser and a real terminal. You prove that a release is immutable, see who uploaded it and from where, and compare its SHA-1 checksum with the file you download yourself. You narrow the deployer role until a delete is refused with a 403. Then you keep the repository healthy: a task that trims old snapshots, a task that compacts the blob store and really frees the disk, a cleanup policy for a proxy cache, a scheduled database backup, a soft quota, and the status checks you can hand to a load balancer.

Honest notes: the browser and terminal are a real continuous recording of Sonatype Nexus Repository Community Edition 3.98.1 on a throwaway lab server, continuing from Lessons 173 and 174. Passwords shown belong to that throwaway server. In the terminal, a small helper named keep prints only the lines that matter from the Maven output, and nx is a short alias for curl with the deployer login. The coloured boxes and labels on top are overlays added for teaching. Because tasks finish in a few seconds on this tiny repository, the pauses while they run are real but short. The narration is synthetic speech. The plan, concept, mistakes, your-turn and recap screens are labelled illustrations, not captures. Use your own machine only.
